Why the sign is where most converters break

A bank statement writes an outgoing payment in at least five different ways, and they do not all mean the same thing to a spreadsheet:

  • -64.28 — a plain minus sign.
  • 64.28 DR — the bank's own debit marker, no minus.
  • (123.45) — accounting parentheses, meaning negative.
  • 100.00 CR — a credit, so positive.
  • −64.28 — the Unicode minus (U+2212) some PDFs use instead of the hyphen.
  • Split layouts — date, description, amount and balance printed on four separate lines.

Invert a minus twice and a $64 purchase becomes a $64 deposit, which quietly doubles the error: the total is off by $128 by the time you reconcile. This tool reads the marker, the parentheses and any minus sign that is already there, and applies the sign once. It also groups multi-line blocks before reading them, so the running balance stays attached to the transaction it belongs to.

Statements that are scans or phone photos have no text layer to read. The tool says so instead of returning an empty table, because a silent blank file is worse than an honest error.
