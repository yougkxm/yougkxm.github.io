How the Offsite Ads fee works

Offsite Ads is the one Etsy cost that can be larger than the transaction and processing fees together, and it arrives only on some orders — which makes it easy to both over-count and under-count.

The rate

  • 15% of the order total for a shop that has always made under $10,000 in any consecutive 365 days — and that shop may opt out.
  • 12% once the shop has made $10,000 or more in such a period — participation is then required for the life of the shop.
  • Charged on item price + shipping the buyer pays + gift wrap, not on US sales tax.

The cap

  • The fee for any one order never exceeds $100, whatever the order total.
  • At 15% the cap only starts at an order total of $666.67; at 12%, at $833.33.
  • So for normal order sizes the cap changes nothing — it protects large orders, not small margins.

Attribution

  • A buyer clicks an offsite ad and orders within 30 days: the order can carry the fee.
  • If the buyer's last click before ordering was an Etsy ad on the site, only the Etsy Ads fee applies.
  • One click can lead to several attributed orders in that window, and no purchase means no fee.

Why the fee is not 15% of your profit — it is worse

Take a $60 item with no shipping charged, $20 of product cost and $6 of postage. Etsy's listing, transaction and processing fees come to about $6.15, which leaves $27.85 before any advertising. A 15% Offsite Ads fee is $9.00, so the attributed order actually pays $18.85 — the "15% fee" has taken roughly a third of the profit. On a thinner product the same fee can turn a good order into a loss, which is why the calculator shows net profit on the attributed order and not only the fee.

Price for the blended cost, not the worst order

The fee is charged only on attributed orders, so raising every price to cover it punishes the buyers who arrived for free. Set the attributed share (the percentage of your orders that carry the fee) and the calculator spreads the fee across the whole shop: that blended number is the true cost of being in the programme, and it is what your price has to cover. If 20% of orders are attributed at 15%, the blended cost is 3% of an order total — about the same as Etsy's transaction and processing fees combined.

After a refund

Refunding through Shop Manager recalculates the transaction and processing fees on the reduced amount. Etsy's help pages do not spell out what happens to the Offsite Ads fee on a seller refund, and one case is documented the other way: when Etsy itself covers a full refund, the transaction and Offsite Ads fees are not credited back. Check the order's fee lines in your Payment account after a refund rather than assuming the fee came back.

Questions

Is the Offsite Ads fee 15% or 12%?

It depends on your shop's revenue in any consecutive 365-day period. A shop that has always stayed under $10,000 is charged 15% and may opt out of the programme. Once a shop has made $10,000 or more in any such period, the fee drops to 12%, and participation becomes required for the lifetime of the shop. The calculator lets you switch between both rates.

Is there a budget or a monthly cap?

No. Offsite Ads has no budget setting: you are charged only when a click turns into an order, and the fee for any single order never exceeds $100, however large the order. At 15% the cap starts to matter at an order total of $666.67; at 12% it starts at $833.33. For ordinary orders the cap never applies.

Which orders count as attributed?

An order where the buyer clicked one of Etsy's offsite ads and bought within 30 days. If the buyer's last click before ordering was an Etsy ad on the site instead, only the Etsy Ads fee applies. One click can lead to several attributed orders inside that 30-day window, and each can carry the fee. There is no fee when a click does not end in a purchase.

What is the fee charged on?

The order total before the advertising fee — item price, shipping the buyer pays and gift wrap. For US sellers it is not charged on sales tax. That is why the calculator has a separate field for shipping the buyer pays, and why the fee looks larger than 15% of your item price when you charge shipping.

Do I need an account or an email address to use this?

No. The page runs entirely in your browser: nothing you type is sent anywhere, there is no signup, and it works offline once loaded.
